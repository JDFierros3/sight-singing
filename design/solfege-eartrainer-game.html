Ear-Trainer Arcade

A gamified skin over the Ear room's three drills, in top-down retro-Frogger style. The stepping stones are the shape notes themselves, sitting on a staff that scrolls right → left. Hear the pitches, tap the right interval, and your character hops onto the next note. Two ways to die: hop wrong and you land off the staff into the water; hop too slow and the scroll carries your note off the left edge with you on it. Auto-plays, ramps with difficulty, keeps a local high score. The base drills are the source of truth; this is a second way to play them.

1 · Concept

Same ear skill, arcade wrapper

Every arcade round is one turn of the underlying drill — but instead of "Play → answer → Reveal", the pitches play automatically and the answer is the move. The staff scrolls right→left beneath a top-down character; the shape notes on it are the only safe footing. Tap the right interval and the character hops onto the next note; tap wrong and it hops off into the water. And the clock is the scroll itself — sit still and your note reaches the left edge and is gone. No Play button, no Reveal — just listen and leap. It's the interactive answer model (see the base Ear room) turned into a continuous, speeding-up run.

Sequencing per drill. Interval Run plays the two pitches sequentially (Do, then the target). Cluster Leap plays them simultaneously (a stack) and you sing the notes apart with the mic to hop onto each. Chord Quest plays a triad and you leap by its quality. All three share the pad-hopping playfield, HUD, ramp, and high score.

Direction: new notes enter from the right and the whole staff scrolls right → left. The character must keep hopping rightward (correct answers) just to stay clear of the encroaching left edge — like Frogger's timer made physical. Retro top-down look: chunky pixels, a simple sprite, the staff as the lane. "Frogger" = the hop-or-die stakes and the scroll-clock, not literal lily pads — the pads ARE the shape-note heads.
2 · Core loop

Listen · leap · survive

BeatWhat happens
1 · AnchorThe current pad sounds its pitch (the reference — Do, or wherever the character stands). The character idles on it.
2 · PromptThe next pitch plays (Interval Run: after the anchor; Cluster Leap: together with it). The next pad is still hidden/ghosted.
3 · Answer buttonsThe interval choices for the current difficulty appear. A shrinking timer bar counts down (the ramp shortens it over time).
4 · LeapTap a choice → the character arcs to the pad that choice implies. Correct → it lands, the pad reveals its shape + syllable, the staff scrolls one step, score +1, a short combo builds. Wrong → it arcs to the wrong pad, the pad crumbles, it falls into the water → −1 life.
5 · RampEvery N correct leaps, tempo and answer-timer speed up a notch and the option set may widen (see Difficulty ramp).
· Two deathsA life is lost either way: hop wrong (land in the water) or hop too slow so the scroll carries your current note into the left edge. The answer timer and the left edge are the same clock.
6 · EndLives reach 0 → Game Over card with the run's score vs. the local high score.
No scoring in the base drills stays true — the arcade is an opt-in mode with its own score/high-score. Switching to Arcade is a deliberate choice, never the default surface.

4 · Cluster Leap — sing it apart

Sing the stack

The microphone IS the controller. A stack of 2–4 pitches sounds together; you turn the mic on and sing the individual pitches out of the chord. Each time your voice lands on one of the notes that's actually sounding, the character hops onto that shape-note pad and it lights up. Pick the whole stack apart — by ear and voice — before the scroll reaches the left edge. This is exactly the harmonic-hearing skill the base "Pitch Distinction" drill trains, turned into a sing-to-move game.

Notes

Mic required. Cluster Leap needs the microphone — starting a round prompts to enable it. Each sung pitch is matched (within tolerance) against the sounding notes; a hit hops the character onto that shape-note and marks it found. Difficulty = stack size (2 → 4) and how close in pitch the notes sit. No-mic fallback: if the singer declines the mic, this drill falls back to tapping the count (2 · 3 · 4) so the room still works.

5 · Chord Quest — simultaneous

Leap by quality

A triad sounds together; tap its quality to leap onto the matching pad, which reveals Do–Mi–Sol (or the minor/dim/aug spelling). This reuses Cluster Leap's simultaneous playfield with quality chips instead of counts.

MajorDo–Mi–Sol MinorDo–Me–Sol DimDo–Me–Se AugDo–Mi–Si
Quality option set scales with difficulty: Easy = Major/Minor; Medium adds Dim; Hard adds Aug + 7ths. Mirrors the base Chord Quality "Test me" chips.
6 · Difficulty ramp

It speeds up so you don't have to

The chosen level sets the starting option-set and pace; the ramp then tightens the screws within the run so a good player keeps getting pushed. No manual re-selecting between rounds — that's the whole point vs. the base drill.

Every…Ramp effect
+5 correctPlayback tempo +1 notch; the answer timer bar shortens ~10%.
+10 correctOption set widens by one (Easy 3 → adds a 4th interval, etc.), up to the level's cap.
Combo ×NScore multiplier grows; a broken combo resets the multiplier but not the ramp.
After a missRamp eases back one notch (mercy) but lives don't refill.
7 · Scoring & high score

One number, stored locally

Score = correct leaps × current combo multiplier. The only persisted stat is the best score per drill × difficulty, in localStorage alongside the rest of the app (zero backend, per-device).

KeyShape
solfege.v1.arcade{ interval:{easy,medium,hard,expert}, cluster:{…}, quality:{…} }
Consistent with the app's storage rule (see the main design system's "no backend" callout): high scores are per-device, cleared with site data, never synced. This does not introduce scoring into the base drills — it lives only in Arcade mode's own namespace.

10 · Build notes

How it plugs in

Reuse, don't rebuild

  • Pitch/interval/cluster/chord generation: the existing js/exercises/* engines + DIFFICULTY_PRESETS — the arcade only changes presentation and pacing.
  • Shape-note pads: the same js/rendering/shapes.js heads + SHAPE_COLORS.
  • Playback: the oscillator/instrument path already used by the drills.
  • Cluster Leap's mic: the existing js/pitch/detection.js autocorrelation + tolerance — a sung pitch within tolerance of a sounding cluster note counts as "found".
  • Voice tuning: pitches sit in the singer's range via the voice-tuning contract (main doc).

New surface

  • A Practice ⇄ Arcade toggle in the Ear room header (Practice is default).
  • An arcade module: game loop (rAF), pad/frog canvas-or-DOM layer, answer buttons bound to the drill's option set, ramp state, lives, combo.
  • High score in solfege.v1.arcade.
  • Respects reduced-motion: the leap arc + scroll can degrade to instant steps.

Sequencing. Ship order: finish the base Ear room's interactive answer buttons (Practice mode) on desktop + mobile, complete the rest of the UI overhaul, then add Arcade as an additive mode. Nothing here should block or complicate the base overhaul. This doc is the spec for that later phase.
